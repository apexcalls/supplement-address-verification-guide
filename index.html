How to Verify a Supplement Brand's Address Is Real

Every legitimate supplement brand has a real address somewhere. It may be printed on the bottle, listed on the contact page, or buried in the terms and conditions. Checking it takes about five minutes, and it is one of the fastest ways to separate a real company from a website that only looks like one.

Start with the address itself. A real business address has a street number, a street name, a city and a postcode. A PO box on its own is not proof of anything — it can be rented by anyone, anywhere, and it tells you nothing about where the product is made or stored. A vague line like "United States" or "California, USA" is even less useful. If a brand will not tell you where it is, ask yourself why.

Next, check the address on a map. Paste it into a maps service and look at what is actually there. A warehouse, an office building or a light-industrial unit is what you would expect. A residential house is not automatically a problem — many small brands start from home — but a petrol station, an empty field or a mail-forwarding shop should make you pause. Street View can be surprisingly revealing: a "headquarters" that turns out to be a virtual office is a fact worth knowing before you spend money.

Then check whether the address matches the rest of the brand. The address on the bottle, the address on the website and the address in the company's registration should agree. In the United States, most companies are registered with a state authority, and those records are public. A mismatch does not always mean fraud — companies move, and paperwork lags — but a brand whose addresses point in three different directions is a brand that has not bothered to keep its story straight.

Finally, look for a human being at the other end. A real company has a phone number that is answered, an email address that gets replies, and a named person or team behind the brand. A website with only a contact form and no address, no phone number and no company name is not a business you can hold to account if something goes wrong. The guarantee on the bottle is only as good as the company behind it.
